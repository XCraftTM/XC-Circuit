# Changelog

All notable changes to XC™ Circuit. Versions follow [semantic versioning](https://semver.org); while in Early Access (0.x), a new minor version may change how things work. Projects are upgraded automatically when they open.

## 0.3.1

### Fixed
- **Older graphics cards draw about twice as fast**: on a card that can't do DirectX 12 or Vulkan but works with OpenGL (such as a Radeon HD 5000), XC Circuit now chooses Older OpenGL by itself instead of OpenGL: on a Mobility Radeon HD 5000 it drew about twice as many frames. If it chose OpenGL before, pick Older OpenGL in Settings › Window › Graphics (or Automatic, to let it choose again).
- **Big workspaces draw faster**: every frame, the canvas worked some things out again for every block, also those off screen: a map of all pins, whether an input comes from the event (rebuilding a list of all wires each time), the project's data written out as text to see whether it changed, and a copy of the Problems list. These are now made with a faster method, kept while nothing changes, or shared instead of copied. The canvas also kept laying out every text again each frame to keep it sharp when zoomed, looked through all the wires for every field to see whether it's connected, and the Workspaces list counted the events of every workspace each frame for its hover text: all of that is now kept until something changes. On a laptop-sized window, a canvas frame takes about a quarter less time.

## 0.3.0

The biggest release so far: your project can live in a Git repository and on a server of your own, several people can work in it at the same time, and the window is laid out anew.

- **Git, built in** (Share › Git…): keep a project on GitHub, GitLab or anywhere that speaks Git, on all your computers and with others. Store, Fetch, and what both sides changed is put together block by block.
- **The XC Circuit Host**: a small server you run yourself. It keeps your projects, carries your secrets to your other devices (sealed on the devices), relays live sessions and can run your bot.
- **Live sessions**: work in one project together, live. One hosts, the others join with an invitation and may change what the host lets them.
- **A new window**: a start window for your projects, real windows for setting things up, a side bar with views for Git, Search and Plugins, notifications, and menus sorted by what they are for.
- **Projects have a new layout** on disk, made for sharing. They are converted when opened; **an older XC Circuit can't open them afterwards**, so update every computer you use a project on.

### Added
- **The docs' pictures are made by the app itself**: the README's picture of the window and the tutorials' pictures of New project, Run and the Plugins menu are new, and come from a test that draws the window without a screen (`cargo test -p circuit-editor docs_screenshots -- --ignored`), so they can be made again whenever the window changes.
- **More for AI agents**: `get_git` and `git_action` (what waits in the project's repository, Store with a few words on what changed, Fetch), `set_workspace` (rename a workspace, move it to a folder, turn it off or on), `delete_workspace` (asks you in the app first), `undo` (and redo) and `notify_user` (a notification in the app). The tools for the bot's remote are called `get_remote` and `remote_action` now, as the interface says Remote; `get_server` and `server_action` still work. For plugins: `xc.remote()` (with `xc.server()` kept), `xc.git()` and `xc.deleteWorkspace(key)`.
- **Read From Message** (Discord): one block that takes a thing out of a message, picked step by step. First what to read from: its text, an embed, its buttons and menus, its layout (Components V2), an attachment, or who it mentions. Then the steps that belong to that: which embed (first to tenth, last, or every one) and its title, description, colour, author, footer, picture, a field by name or number, or all its text; which row and which button or menu, and its label, custom id, link, emoji or a menu's options; a layout piece by piece: where to look (the whole message or one container), what kind of piece (a text, section, container, media gallery, picture, button, select menu or file, or all its text at once), which one (first to tenth, last, or every one), and what of it (a section's texts and the button or picture beside it; a container's colour, text and number of pieces; a gallery's pictures; a button's label, custom id, link, emoji and style; a menu's placeholder, options and what it picks; a file's URL and name); which attachment and its URL, name, size or type. A last step on any text gives it as it is, or only the user, role or channel mentions in it, its links, numbers, custom emoji, lines or words, or what matches a pattern (the first match, every match, or the first group). The steps that don't belong to what you picked are hidden, the result has the right type for the next block, and **Found** says whether the message had that part.
- **Ctrl+Shift+T brings back the tab closed last**, as in a browser: a workspace, a page or a panel comes back where it was among its area's tabs, and a builder (or the bot's settings) with what you were editing in it, so closing one by mistake loses nothing. Press it again for the one before; the last 25 are remembered until the project closes. Also File › Reopen Closed Tab, and it can be changed in Settings › Shortcuts.
- **Open the project's folder**: File › Open Project Folder, the folder button at the top of the Workspaces view, or the command palette shows the project's files in the system's file manager.
- **Ctrl+W closes the tab in front, Ctrl+1 to Ctrl+9 switch tabs**, as in a browser. Ctrl+W (also File › Close Tab) closes the workspace, builder or page you are in, as its × does; panels such as the Console stay. It can be changed in Settings › Shortcuts. Ctrl+1 to Ctrl+9 go to that tab of the area you are in (the workspaces' tabs, or the bottom panels' while you are in one), and Ctrl+9 to its last one.
- **Move and fold the side bar's views by their name**: drag a view by its name at the top of the side bar onto the lower half to show it under another one, onto the activity bar to put its icon elsewhere, or out into the window to make it a tab. With two views shown, a click on a name folds that view away to its heading, the line between them is easier to take hold of, and a double-click on it gives each half the height.
- **Plugins can show notifications**: `xc.notify(message, level?)` shows a card in the window's corner and keeps it behind the bell; `xc.warn` and `xc.error` do so already.
- **Notifications**: what is worth a look shows for a few seconds as a card in the window's bottom right corner and is kept behind the bell at the end of the status bar, which counts the unread ones. A block that fails while the bot runs comes with **Show block**, other errors and warnings with Show the Console; how storing in Git and uploading went is told there too, and what is under way (saving, storing, uploading, downloading an update) shows with a spinner or a bar. The same error again counts up instead of piling up. The list has Clear all and Do not disturb, which keeps everything in the list without showing it.
- **See which blocks run on the remote**: with Remote picked beside Run, the canvas lights up the blocks that run on a Calagopus server or on your Host, with how long they took and where one failed, as it does for a bot run from the editor. The bot only reports while the editor watches, hides its secrets itself, and sends what goes through the wires only if you turn that on in Bot › Remote…. Upload the bot once with this version for it.
- **Live sessions (Share › Live session…, a first version)**: work in one project together, at the same time. One person hosts: the project stays theirs, is saved at theirs, and the bot runs there. Guests join with an invitation, see every change as it happens, and may change what the host ticks for each of them: slash commands, workspaces, blocks everywhere or in some workspaces. Changes are put together block by block, Undo only takes back your own work, and everything is sealed between the apps: the relay in between (the public one, or your own Host) can't read it. You see the others' pointers and what they have selected, each in their colour; guests read the bot's console, see which blocks ran, and can start and stop the bot at the host's if the host lets them. Previews show the bot's name and picture for everyone, and a guest the host ticks it for picks channels and roles from the bot's servers, read by the host: the token stays with the host. Plugins the host has and a guest doesn't are passed on (and asked about before they load), and a guest whose connection drops gets back in by itself, as who they were. See `Live sessions: working together` in the docs.
- **The XC Circuit Host** (`host/`, a first version): a small server to run yourself in Docker, behind your reverse proxy. **Share › Host…** pairs your computer with it (the first with a code from the host's log, every other with a code a paired device shows), keeps a project there, and sends and gets a project's secrets: the bot token arrives on your other devices without typing it in again, sealed on your devices so the host can't read it. It runs the bot too: choose Your host in Bot › Remote…, and the host installs the bot's packages, starts it and keeps it online without your computer; Run, Restart, Stop and the live console work from the editor. Live sessions can meet there instead of at the public relay. See `The Host: a place of your own` in the docs.
- **Git, built in (Share › Git…)**: keep a project in a repository on GitHub, GitLab, Gitea or any other place that speaks Git over HTTPS, to have it on several computers and to work on it with others. **Store** saves the project, records what changed and sends it; **Fetch** gets what is stored there and puts it together with yours. Once connected it keeps in step by itself: every save is stored, and what others stored comes in. Two people can work in the same workspace: changes are put together block by block and wire by wire, two blocks that got the same number both stay (one is renumbered, with what reads it), and only the same setting changed two ways is asked about, block by block: yours, the stored one, or both. A guide in the dialog leads through the setup. Sign in with GitHub in the browser (you type in a short code), or paste an access token; the sign-in stays in your system's credential store. **Earlier versions** lists what was stored and lets you go back to any of them, and Store stops when a block holds something that looks like a secret (a webhook address, a token). A project kept in Git needs no folder of your own: New Project offers **In a Git repository**, and XC Circuit keeps the folder itself; **Disconnect…** lets go of the repository again. **File › Get from Git…** (also on the start page) brings a stored project onto another computer, and the status bar shows what waits to be stored or fetched. You don't need Git installed, and the bot token, passwords and the `.circuit/` folder never go there. See `Keeping a project in Git` in the docs.
- **`${#4.…}` is checked, and a block's problems show on the block**: a text can read another block's output by its number, such as `${#4.message}`. That only gives something when block 4 ran before on the same path, and until now a mistake showed up when the bot ran, as an error. XC Circuit now checks every such reference while you build: whether the block is there, whether it runs before the block that reads it (not after it, and not on another event's path), and whether it has an output of that name (`${#4.mesage}` is caught, with the outputs it does have). What Problems says about a block also stands under the block on the canvas, in amber, so you see it where you work.

### Changed
- **More of the window is yours to arrange, and a new look to start with**: a fourth button at the right of the title bar, **Customize Layout**, ticks every part of the window (activity bar, side bar, bottom panels, right panels, status bar) and puts the side bar on the left or the right. The bottom panels have two buttons at the end of their tab bar: fill the window (and back), and hide. Clicking a plugin in the Plugins view opens its page as a tab: what it is, who made it, how many blocks it adds in which categories, its commands and panels, and where its folder is. A new theme, **Modern Dark** (flat near-black with a blue accent), is the one a fresh install starts with; your own choice stays, and the gear's Themes menu switches.
- **The side bar works like an editor's**: each view has its name on top with its own small buttons and a ⋯ menu, and is made of sections that fold away under a heading with a count. **Git** is a view of its own there: a line for what changed, **Store** and **Fetch**, the changed workspaces and commands by name, and the stored versions as a line of dots (right-click one to go back to it); setting it up and signing in stay in Git's window. **Plugins** lists what you installed and what is built in, with a search, a picture, what each does, who made it, and a gear for turning it off, removing it or showing its folder. A new **Search** view finds workspaces and blocks by name, number or what is typed into them, block by block under their workspace. The Git and Problems icons in the activity bar show a count, and a right-click on the bar ticks what is an icon in it.
- **A start window, and a tidier editor window**: without a project, XC Circuit is now a small start window: your recent projects with a search, New Project, Open and Get from Git, and pages for joining a live session, your Host, plugins, learning (the tour, the example bot, the docs) and the settings. Opening a project turns it into the editor's window, where and as big as you left it; File › Close Project brings the start window back.
- **Setup opens in real windows, builders in tabs**: New project, Export, Bot › Remote… and importing or exporting workspaces open in a window of their own beside the editor's (it can go on another monitor), no longer in a window floating inside it. The Embed, Layout, Modal and Canvas Builders, Slash Commands and Bot settings open as tabs beside the workspaces, and the remote's console with the panels at the bottom.
- **Menus, status bar and activity bar sorted by what they are for**: Git, Host and Live session have their own menu, **Share** (they were under File), and Git and Live session start as icons in the activity bar. The status bar only says how things stand: the bot, Git, problems and the live session on the left; Saved, the last upload, the kind of bot and updates on the right. The plugin count and the theme are gone from it; themes are in the new menu of the gear at the bottom of the activity bar, with the command palette, settings, plugins and updates. Three buttons at the right of the title bar hide and show the side bar, the bottom panels and the right panels.
- **"Server" is now "Remote"** (in German: "Host"): the place your bot is uploaded to and runs on was called its server, the same word Discord uses for a community. It is now Bot › Remote…, Upload to the remote, Remote console, and the Local | Remote switch beside Run. Nothing about how it works changed; a Calagopus panel's servers keep their name.
- **Projects have a new layout, made for sharing and syncing**: a project folder now keeps apart what the bot is and what is yours alone. `circuit.project.json` holds the name, the kind of bot and the shared settings; each slash command has its own file in `commands/`; and where the bot is uploaded to, where its databases are and other private settings are in `.circuit/local.json`, which stays out of Git. So a project can be shared or put into a repository without your server's address in it, and two people changing different commands change different files. Every project also gets an id of its own. **Your projects are brought to the new layout when you open them**, after a backup; the old `circuit.json` is kept in `.circuit/layout-1/`. An XC Circuit older than 0.3.0 can't open a project once it has the new layout.
- **Private settings stay on your computer**: some of Bot › Settings is about you rather than the bot: the id of your test server, the client ids that go with your Twitch, Kick or Spotify keys, the addresses of the Server Tools (SSH, RCON, Docker), and the switches that allow blocks to reach servers and databases. These are no longer saved in the shared project file, so a shared or synced project neither shows them nor turns such a switch on for someone else. The bot gets them as before. Plugins mark their own settings with `private = true`.
- **Moving or renaming a project's folder keeps its token and backups**: the bot token, other saved secrets and the backups went by where the folder is, so a moved or renamed project asked for its token again and started without its backups. They go by the project's id now and come along. A copy of a project folder is a project of its own to them: it doesn't get the original's token while the original is still there.
- **FTP is gone from Bot › Server…**: plain FTP sends the password and the bot token unprotected, and FTPS was rarely on offer where SFTP wasn't. Uploading works over SFTP or through a Calagopus panel. A project that was set to FTP says so in Bot › Server… and uploads nothing until you choose one of the two.

### Fixed
- **A note looks the same at every zoom**: zoomed out, a note's title and description were drawn bigger to stay readable, so its heading grew down over the blocks in it and the note changed shape with every zoom step. The heading is now a fixed part of the note that gets smaller and bigger with the blocks, and the edit button no longer grows past the title's line (a double-click on the title edits the note at any zoom).
- **Emoji sit on the line of the text around them**: colour emoji such as ✅ and 🎫 were drawn standing on the letters' line and taller than them, so beside text they looked lifted. They are drawn a little lower now, with their middle at the middle of the capitals, in blocks' fields, tab names and everywhere else.
- **A plugin's own blocks show in the block picker**: the picker only offered blocks of plugins that the kind of bot (Discord Bot, Fluxer Bot) lists, so the blocks of a plugin you wrote or installed never showed there unless you edited the Discord plugin itself. A plugin that adds blocks now has them offered in every kind of bot; one whose blocks only fit one kind names it in its plugin.toml (`[contributes] blockModes = ["discord-bot"]`). Discord's own blocks still stay out of Fluxer bots, and the other way round.
- **The search box in the title bar no longer lies under Local | Remote**: it had a fixed width in the middle of the title bar, so in a narrower window, or with Local | Remote beside Run, the buttons were drawn over it. It now takes the room between the menus and the buttons: narrower when there is less, moved off the middle when one side needs more, and left out when there is none (Ctrl+P still opens the search).
- **Notes under a block stay inside the block's width**: a failed block's error was written on one long line that ran over the wire and the next block, and zoomed out, the words of errors and warnings spilled out of their box. They now wrap to the block's width, and zoomed far out a plain red or yellow bar marks the block until you zoom in.
- **Stored values named like `constructor` or `toString` work like any other**: every JavaScript object answers to a few names by itself. Get Stored Value gave a function instead of the fallback for a name such as `constructor` that was never stored, and Has Stored Value said yes. A value stored as `__proto__` wasn't kept at all, and what was in it then showed up under the bucket's other names. That mattered where the name comes from what people type (a tag command, say). Only names that were really stored count now.
- **An exported bot reads secrets with a `#` or quotes in them correctly**: the export wrote each secret into `.env` as it is, but the bot reads ` #…` as a comment and takes quotes off. A database password such as `#secret1` came back empty, `pa ss #1` as `pa ss`, and the bot then failed to log in with no hint why. Such values are written in quotes now, and read back whole. A `.env` you filled in yourself is read as before.
- **An exported bot no longer carries your server and your folders**: `index.js` held everything of Bot › Server… (address, port, user name, folder) and, for each block, the full path of its file on your computer, which has your Windows user name in it. Neither is needed to run the bot, and exported bots get shared. Both are left out now; blocks are named by their plugin and file (`xc.core/blocks/debug/log.js`).
- **One block's start-up mistake no longer keeps the whole bot from starting**: when a block failed while setting itself up, the bot stopped with that error. The Console now says which block failed, and the rest of the bot starts.
- **Discord bots: an error from the connection no longer ends the bot**: discord.js reports some problems as an `error` on the client, and with nobody listening Node.js ends the program. It's written to the Console now, as Fluxer bots already did.
- **An exported bot starts again after a crash**: when an exported bot ended on an error nothing was there to catch, it stayed off until someone noticed, and the Restart block only stopped it. `node index.js` now keeps watch over the bot: after a crash it starts it again (5 seconds later, longer if it keeps crashing right away), and a restart the bot asks for happens at once. A bot that can't start at all (no token, a wrong one) isn't started over and over. `node index.js --no-restart` (or `XC_NO_RESTART=1`) runs it as before, for those who use pm2, systemd or a panel for this.

## 0.2.10

### Changed
- **Saving writes only the files that changed**: every save wrote every workspace's file again, also the ones nothing had changed in. Those are left alone now, so saving a big project is quicker, and tools that go by a file's date (Git, backups, sync folders) see only what really changed.
- **npm packages stay installed when you switch bots**: all bots share one packages folder, and it held only the packages of the bot that ran last. Running another bot with other packages (a Fluxer bot after a Discord bot, one with a database, one with music) removed the first one's, and going back installed them again, every time. Packages now stay once installed; where two bots ask for different versions of one package, the bot that runs gets its own.

### Security
- **XC Circuit asks before it loads a project's own plugins**: a project can bring plugins in its `plugins/` folder, and they loaded as soon as it opened. With a project from someone else that was their code running on your computer, without a click: a plugin's editor script could start the bot, which runs the plugin's blocks with the same access as any program. Now **Load this project's plugins?** lists them first, once for each project folder and plugin; your answer is kept on your computer, not in the project. With Not now the project opens without them (their blocks show as not installed), and Plugins › Load This Project's Plugins… asks again. The example bot needs no asking. A project's plugin can no longer take the place of one that comes with XC Circuit (such as Discord Bot, which handles your token). After updating, your own projects with a `plugins/` folder ask once.
- **A plugin can't start the bot by itself**: the script of a plugin that doesn't come with XC Circuit can start the bot or act on its server (`xc.run("bot.run")`, `server.…`, `run.…`) only from one of its commands or from a click on its button, link or overlay, not from a timer, a check or while it draws.
- **The Console hides the bot's secrets**: a bot can be made to print its own token or a saved login (a Log block with `${process.env…}` is enough), and it then stood in the Console, where a screen share shows it, a plugin's script reads it and an AI agent gets it with `get_console`. Wherever one of the bot's secrets turns up in what the bot prints, in a block's values on a wire or in an error, `[hidden secret]` now stands instead, also when only its start shows. This is a safeguard, not a wall: code in your bot still has the token, as it must.
- **AI agents: clearer limits, said as they are**: the script an agent runs with `run_script` no longer gets the part of the plugin API that adds XC Circuit to AI clients' settings, nor the functions behind it (see below). The panel and the docs no longer say an agent "never sees" the token: no tool gives it out and the Console hides it, but an agent that builds and runs your bot works with a bot that has it.
- **Editor plugins can't read files outside their own folder** (Windows): a plugin's script loads its own files with `require("./file.js")`, and shows its own pictures and plays its own sounds. A name holding a drive, such as `./C:notes/keys.txt`, got past the check that keeps those inside the plugin's folder, so a plugin (or an AI agent, through `run_script`) could read text files elsewhere on the computer. Such names are refused now, and so is a link in the folder that points outside it. The functions behind `xc` are no longer there for a script to call directly.
- **Your server password, API key and bot token only go to the server you saved**: where a bot is uploaded to is kept in the project, so it comes along with a project from someone else, changes with a Git pull, and could be changed by a plugin's script or an AI agent; the password, key and token are kept on your computer. They went to whatever server the project named: another address got your saved password and, with Send the bot token on, the token. XC Circuit now remembers the server you saved in Bot › Server… for each project on this computer. When the project names another one, nothing is sent, the Console says so, and Bot › Server… shows a note: look it over and press Save to use it. Scripts and agents can no longer change the server, and `get_server` tells agents when it isn't the saved one (`savedHere`). After updating, a project with a saved password or key keeps working as it is; one that uploads with a key file alone asks you to press Save once.
- **HTTP Request's "public web only" holds for IPv6 addresses too**: an address from `${…}` or a wire may only go to the public web, not to the bot's own computer or network. Written as an IPv6 address with an IPv4 one inside it (`http://[::ffff:127.0.0.1]/`, or a cloud's `[::ffff:169.254.169.254]`), a private address counted as public. Addresses are now compared by their numbers, however they're written, including the older ways of putting an IPv4 address into an IPv6 one.
- **Links from plugins only open web pages**: a block's Docs button, and a build mode's invite page and developer portal, handed whatever text the plugin gave to the system to open, which opens a program or a file just as readily as a web page. They open `https://` and `http://` addresses only now; with anything else the button is greyed out and shows the text when you point at it.
- **The AI agents' connection is harder to get into**: the secret an AI client's connection needs is kept in a file only you can read (on Linux it could be read by other users of the computer, who could then steer the editor), and comes from the system's own random numbers. A program that connects and doesn't say the secret within 5 seconds is let go, a line without an end is cut off, and at most 32 connections are open at once.
- **An update is checked again just before it's installed**: a downloaded update waits on the disk until you close XC Circuit, and was then run as it was, from the temporary folder every program writes to. It's now kept in XC Circuit's data folder and checked against its checksum once more before it runs; a file that changed meanwhile is deleted instead. The app only updates itself with a file from its own releases on GitHub that has a checksum; otherwise it opens the release page, as before for other kinds of install.
- **A plugin's zip can't unpack to more than a plugin may be**: a small zip can claim to hold gigabytes, which would have filled the memory and the disk while it was installed. Installing stops at 64 MB for one file, 256 MB in all or 20,000 files (the bundled plugins are a few megabytes each), and leaves nothing behind.
- **A plugin only reads its own files**: the patterns in a plugin's `plugin.toml`, the files a block brings into the bot (`requires.assets`), a block's own icon and a build mode's runtime libraries could name files outside the plugin's folder, which then went into the bot and into exports. Those are left out now, with a line in Problems saying which.

### Fixed
- **The search (Ctrl+P) no longer stops the app on Turkish text**: looking for a word that stands near the end of a block's text with several İ in it closed XC Circuit. Such texts are searched like any other now, and typing `indirim` finds İndirim.
- **A panel too narrow for its controls no longer keeps the app busy**: a row with a dropdown, a slider or a number that didn't fit its panel (a plugin's panel in a narrow side bar) made XC Circuit draw itself again without end, which costs processor and battery for nothing. So did a settings page with two settings of the same name. Both come to rest now.
- **Release notes: square brackets before a link stay text**: in the update window, a line such as `An array [a, b] … see [the docs](…)` turned everything from the first bracket on into the link. Only the link is one now.
- **A Try Zone's border has the colour it was meant to have**: it was drawn about 40% darker than the theme's accent colour, because its see-through-ness was counted twice when colours were mixed. It's brighter now, and glows towards green as before when the zone runs.
- **A number field keeps to its `max` without a `min`**: a block's number field with only a `max` took any number. (None of the bundled blocks has one; blocks of other plugins may.)
- **A dropdown shows what it is really set to**: a dropdown in Bot › Settings or in a plugin's panel whose value was none of its choices (nothing chosen yet, or a choice the plugin no longer has) showed its first choice, as if that were set. It now says **Choose…** when nothing is chosen, and shows a value that isn't one of the choices as it is.
- **The bot's output no longer stops at an odd character**: a line that wasn't UTF-8 (a program the bot starts answering in Windows' own encoding, with an umlaut in it) ended the reading of everything the bot wrote, so the Console stayed empty from there on, blocks stopped lighting up, and the bot could end with a write error. Such characters now show as `�` and the rest is read as before. The same goes for npm's output while packages install, and for the database helper.
- **Installing Node.js can't hang on a talkative installer**: winget's or the installer's output and errors were read one after the other, so one that wrote much to its errors first waited forever. Both are read at the same time now.
- **An AI agent's request that took too long isn't done later after all**: while a dialog was open (a file picker, say), an agent's tool call waited, and after a minute the agent was told it failed. The call still ran once the dialog closed, so an agent that had tried again got its block twice. A call that was given up is now dropped, and the agent is told nothing was done.
- **Workspaces named Con, Nul, Aux, Prn, Com1 or Lpt1 can be saved**: Windows keeps those names for devices, so the workspace's file (`con.json`) couldn't be written and saving failed. Such a workspace now gets a file name of its own (`con-workspace.json`); its name in the side bar stays as typed.
- **Replacing a plugin can't lose it**: installing a plugin's zip over an installed version (or a block update) deleted the old version first; if the new one then couldn't be moved into place, the plugin was gone. The old version now steps aside and comes back when that happens. A folder an interrupted install left behind is no longer taken for a plugin.
- **Stop doesn't leave the bot's helpers running** (Windows): a bot that didn't stop within a moment was ended, but programs it had started (a player or converter for music, say) kept running. They're ended with it now.
- **A power cut right after saving can't leave an empty file**: a saved project file took the old one's place before its contents were surely on the disk, so a power cut at that moment could leave it empty. It's written to the disk first now. The same goes for backups and the crash recovery copy.
- **Block source shows the file instead of running it**: the Inspector's Block source opened a block's `.js` file with whatever Windows opens such files with, which is its script host unless you chose an editor: an error box at best. It now shows the file in its folder, to open with the editor you like.
- **Message previews: a link without a text is no link**: `[](http://…)` was shown as a link (an empty one) while `[](https://…)` stayed as typed. Both stay as typed now, as in Discord.
- **Slash Commands: a pasted command for administrators only stays one**: in Discord's JSON, `"default_member_permissions": "0"` means nobody but administrators can use the command. Pasted into the builder it became a command for everyone. It now comes in with Administrator ticked, which says the same.
- **Slash Commands: editing a command as JSON keeps its folder and its switch**: a command changed in the JSON view left its side bar folder, and one that was turned off was on again, with the first valid edit. Both stay as they were.
- **Slash Commands: Smallest and Largest take negative numbers and decimals**: the boxes wrote the number out again after every key, so a minus typed first vanished and so did the point: `-5.2` ended up as 52. They now keep what you type until the number is whole. A choice's number shows as typed, too (1, not 1.0).
- **Embed Builder: "When it's sent" is no longer lost after another embed had a set time**: once "A set time" was picked for one embed's timestamp, the next embed opened showed "A set time" too, with an empty box, and its own "When it's sent" was gone when you pressed Apply. Each embed keeps its own now.
- **Layout Builder and Modal Builder no longer stop the app on parts that aren't objects**: a message whose media gallery listed its pictures as plain addresses (`"items": ["https://…"]`), or a select menu whose options were plain texts, closed XC Circuit as soon as the builder showed it. Pasted JSON like that is now refused with what's wrong. A block that already holds such a layout (put there by hand or by an AI agent) opens in the JSON view with the same note, instead of an empty builder.
- **Slash Commands and the side bar no longer undo each other**: with Slash Commands open, a click on a command in the side bar read every command afresh and threw away changes you hadn't saved yet. And a command turned off, moved to a folder or dragged elsewhere in the side bar while the builder was open came back as it was with the builder's next Save. Now the click only shows that command, and the open builder takes over what the side bar changes.
- **Block picker: the keyboard works while the mouse rests on the list**: the row under the pointer took the highlight back in every frame. With the pointer over the list (where the picker often opens), the arrow keys did nothing, and Enter after typing a search placed the block under the pointer instead of the best match. A row is now highlighted when the pointer moves onto it.
- **Canvas Builder: a locked layer can't be resized by dragging**: its corner handle was hidden, but dragging where it would be still resized the layer.
- **A wire picked up and dropped on a pin it doesn't fit goes back**: it was deleted, as if dropped on empty canvas. Dropped on empty canvas it is still deleted.
- **New Project reads its starters once**: while the window was open, every starter's files were read from the disk again for each frame drawn (60 times a second), and the folder the project goes in was looked up as often, which could hold the window up with a location on a slow or unplugged network drive. The starters are read when the window opens, the folder when you change the name or location and about once a second.
- **A very long line in the Console no longer slows the app down**: a line of hundreds of thousands of characters (a big JSON printed by the bot, say) was measured again from its start for each row it wraps into, for every frame the Console was drawn. With a line of a million characters that took a tenth of a second per frame. It's done in one pass now.
- **Canvas Builder: the preview reads a colour of digits as the bot does**: a colour written as six digits without `#` (such as `112233`, from a preview value) was shown as another colour than the bot draws, because the preview took it for a plain number. It's read as hex, like the bot does.
- **Layout Builder and Modal Builder: a new part's custom id isn't one that's taken**: new buttons, menus and questions were numbered by how many parts there were, so after removing one the next got an id another already had, and "used twice" showed up right away. They're numbered one above the highest now.
- **Stored Data doesn't save over a store.json it couldn't read**: when the bot's `store.json` was there but couldn't be read (held by another program for a moment, or cut off), the panel showed "Nothing stored yet", and adding a value then saved a file with only that value over everything the bot had stored. The panel now says the file can't be read and changes nothing until it can.
- **The colour picker remembers a colour also when closed with its own button**: a colour showed up under Recent when the picker was closed with Escape or a click elsewhere, but not with a click on the swatch that opened it.
- **Builders' JSON view: Clear no longer leaves "Applied" standing**: after Clear the box was empty, but the line above it still said "Applied" and Apply put the earlier design on the block. An empty box now says "Paste JSON here", and Apply waits for valid JSON.
- **More of the interface is translated**: some texts stayed English in other languages: problems such as "needs a value" and "is not installed", the Backups page's first lines, the block picker's "by … · In: … · Out: …", the Slash Commands builder's notes on names and descriptions, "connect" and "Caught: …" on the canvas, and a few error lines in the Console. They're translated now (German comes with it).

## 0.1.0 – Early Access

The first public release.

### Building bots
- A canvas editor for **Discord** (discord.js) and **Fluxer** (fluxer.js) bots: events start a flow, actions run in order along green wires, values are worked out when an action needs them.
- **242 blocks**: messages, embeds and Components V2 layouts, slash commands with options and subcommands, buttons, select menus and modals, moderation, roles, channels, members, voice and music, stored data, variables, timers, web requests, JSON, text, numbers, lists, dates, and Run Code / Use Method for anything else.
- **JavaScript in every text** with `${…}`, with completion for the values earlier blocks give and their members.
- **Try Zones** that retry, time out and catch errors; an On Error event.
- **Forms** with text boxes, menus, file uploads and text (Components V2), and Modal Submitted gives every answer.
- **Fluxer bots** with 62 blocks: roles, bans, emojis and stickers, webhooks, invites, threads, channel permissions, reactions, Wait For, the audit log, and events for bans, roles, channels, threads, members, voice, typing and more.
- **Clear errors** when the bot is missing a permission: they say which one, and where to give it. Bot settings lists the privileged intents your blocks need, with a link to turn them on.
- **Workspaces** in folders, which can be turned off to leave them out of the bot without deleting them.
- **Notes** to explain and group blocks: title, description, colour, move (with the blocks inside, or alone), resize, copy.
- **Embed, Layout and Slash Command Builders** with live Discord previews and Discord's own JSON.
- **Snap to grid**, **align and spread** selected blocks, **favourite and recent** blocks in the picker.
- **Run with one click** (F5): blocks glow as they run, show how long they took, and failures show on the block. **Hover a wire** to see the last value that went through it.
- **Export** a bot as a folder that runs with `node index.js` anywhere.
- **Starter bots** in New Project: welcome & auto role, moderation, levels & XP, tickets, role menu; basic commands and moderation for Fluxer. **DemoBot** example.

### Plugins
- Blocks are plain **JavaScript or TypeScript** files that reload when saved.
- **Editor plugins** in JavaScript: panels, commands, checks, overlays (a desk pet!), timers, sounds, pictures, placing and selecting blocks. Sandboxed, no approval needed.
- **Native plugins** in Rust with egui, off until allowed.
- Build modes, pin types, themes, translations and starter bots come from plugins.
- **Install plugins from a zip** (Plugins › Install from Zip…, or drop the file on the window).

### Comfort
- Command palette, changeable **keyboard shortcuts**, themes and accent colours, touch mode, interface size, English and German.
- Backups on every save with restore, crash recovery for unsaved changes, and **crash logs with a ready bug report**.
- Update check (Help › Check for Updates), and **block updates** without an app update: newer versions of the bundled blocks download and load right away (Help › Undo Block Updates goes back).
- Installers: Windows Setup (x64 and ARM64), Linux AppImage, .deb, .rpm and .tar.gz.
- A [website](https://xcrafttm.github.io/XC-Circuit/) with tutorials, explanations and the full plugin API.
